## What travel agency software actually covers in 2026

The term "travel agency software" now spans five distinct categories, and most agencies need tools from two or three of them. Confusing the categories is the most common source of buying mistakes.

The five categories are: **trip production and quoting software** (build custom itineraries, price complex trips, generate branded documents), **booking and payment platforms** (accept deposits, split payments, manage group registrations), **CRM and advisor tools** (client database, commission tracking, marketing automation), **GDS and reservation systems** (access airline and hotel inventory), and **traveler-facing apps** (white-label mobile apps, offline itineraries, push notifications).

A solo advisor selling cruises needs a CRM and an itinerary builder. A DMC handling custom group trips in Morocco needs trip production software with supplier management and multi-currency budgeting. A retail agency booking flights through Sabre needs GDS access and back-office accounting. These are three different buying problems.

Travefy dominates the US solo advisor market because it solves the right problem at the right price. New advisors need a polished itinerary, a client CRM, and a professional brand presence without learning five tools. Travefy bundles all three starting at $25 per month for new agents.

**Pros:** low entry price, strong itinerary templates, 200+ supplier integrations, native client mobile app. **Cons:** limited layout customization, English only, no multi-currency or multi-brand logic for international operators. ### Best for group travel and retreats: WeTravel

WeTravel is a payment-first platform built around group trip economics. Its flat 1% processing fee, installment payment plans, and free supplier transfers via the WeTravel Card make it the default choice for retreat leaders, adventure travel companies, and multi-day group operators. The itinerary builder exists but is secondary to the payment engine.

**Pros:** lowest transaction fees in group travel, strong payment plan logic, supplier transfer network. **Cons:** limited CRM depth, non-transparent international fees in some regions, not suited for bespoke FIT trips.

### Best for US-based advisor CRMs: Tern

Tern has raised $17 million in funding and reached over 8,000 advisors by focusing on AI features that save hours per week. Its AI Assist parses supplier PDFs, its AI Notetaker joins advisor calls and updates the CRM automatically, and its commission reconciliation engine auto-matches payments to bookings.

**Pros:** deepest AI integration in advisor CRMs, strong commission tools, modern UI. **Cons:** US-centric, limited value for DMCs or tour operators, no trip production or margin logic.

### Best for white-label traveler apps: mTrip

mTrip is the reference platform for agencies wanting their own branded mobile app in the Apple App Store and Google Play. Travelers search for the agency's name, download the agency's app, and never see mTrip's brand. The 2026 AI Import Wizard parses booking PDFs into structured itineraries automatically.

**Pros:** unmatched white-label depth, strong offline capabilities, integrations with Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport, and mid-office systems. **Cons:** custom pricing often out of reach for small agencies, focused on traveler experience rather than back-office production.

### Best for retail agencies: Sabre, Amadeus, TravelWorks

If your agency sells airline tickets and earns ARC or BSP commissions, Sabre and Amadeus are not optional. These are the two dominant global distribution systems and they power most retail ticketing. Sabre Red 360 is the workspace retail agents use daily; Amadeus Selling Platform Connect plays a similar role in European markets. Neither platform is designed for custom itinerary production or client-facing proposals, which is why retail agencies typically pair them with TravelWorks for back-office accounting and increasingly a trip production layer like Ezus for the non-GDS portion of their business.

### Best for tour operators in English-speaking markets: Tourwriter and Moonstride

Tourwriter serves custom tour operators primarily in New Zealand and Australia. Moonstride serves European mid-size operators with a more ERP-oriented approach including XML supplier connectivity. Both are credible tour operator tools but lack Ezus's multi-brand, multi-language depth for agencies operating across multiple markets.

## How to choose the right travel agency software

Start by mapping your agency against the five software categories. A single tool rarely covers all five, and forcing a one-size-fits-all platform usually leads to a tool that does everything poorly. Most successful agencies in 2026 run a stack of two to four specialized tools connected through APIs.

The three questions that matter most during evaluation are: **What does my team spend the most time on today?** (quote building, payments, client communication, commission reconciliation), **Where does revenue leak from my process?** (margin errors, lost quotes, slow response times, manual data re-entry), and **What do I want travelers to experience?** (branded app, PDF, web portal, generic vendor interface). Match your answers to the categories above to prioritize your stack.
